# 2026-11-02 — "Linear" was said from fixed limits in milliseconds

- **What happened.** #258 wrote in packages/cli/test/dockerfile-check.test.ts that "the lock and Dockerfile readers decide in linear time", and named its tests `gate-reader-linear`. What those tests held was a fixed time at one size of each input: 50 ms, 1 s and 4 s. So the sentence said more than the test, in two ways. It was general, while the same PR's record measured 37,105 ms on one key of about 4,096 characters with 10,000 dependencies and called that "no bound"; `resolveFrom` is quadratic in a key's length, as `lock-key-many-deps` now measures (about 14 times from a key of 1,019 characters to one of 4,034). And a limit at one size does not show how a time grows. On main's code from before #258, `lockEntries` and `lockExtraneous` on 1,000 extraneous keys of 4,096 characters took about 480 ms, under the 1 s limit, so they passed with the quadratic expression still there; from 1,024 characters to 4,096 they grew 12.70 and 12.93 times. The limits also depended on the machine: GitHub's runner went over the first limit on `packageLockInputs`, 1 s, in all 3 tries, and 4 s was then picked "between the two times". A first-principles audit on 2026-11-02 found the sentence and the limits.
- **Why.** The word came from the reasoning behind the fix (an expression with one way to match any key), not from a measurement, and nobody compared it with what the test measures. A limit in milliseconds was the check at hand, as `time-cap-composer-name.test.ts` already used one to stop an exponential expression, which a fixed limit does catch. A quadratic time needs two sizes to be seen.
- **What changed.** `gate-reader-scaling-ratio` measures each reader on the same shape at n and 4n, as the median of up to 7 samples taken in turn, and holds the ratio under 8 (a time that grows with n² gives about 16). `gate-reader-scaling-ratio-control` holds that the same measure gives under 8 on a scan and over 8 on a known n², in the same run. The file's first line and its describe names now say "these shapes". `lock-key-many-deps` writes the quadratic time down as it is, a ratio from 8 up to 32, and calls it neither linear nor fixed; the fix is left to another PR, and when it lands those tests fail. The time limits that remain, 5 s and 30 s for a sample and 15 s for a measure, stop a run that would hang; they do not decide what is linear.
- **What this does not catch.** A ratio at one pair of sizes says how a listed shape grew between those sizes: not how another shape grows, and not a term still too small to show at those sizes. Where a reader refuses a file at its first line, the ratio is low because little of the file is read (the lowest are under 2), which is a true measure and not evidence about the rest of the reader. Sentences outside this PR's files still say "linear": the comment on `LOCK_PATH` in packages/cli/src/security/judge.ts, line 1 of packages/cli/test/time-cap-composer-name.test.ts (which holds a 50 ms limit), and test names in readonly-rust.test.ts, readonly-framework.test.ts and readonly-registered.test.ts. They were not measured here. The ratio can also fail on a busy machine: this PR's description gives how often it did.
- **Check:** `packages/cli/test/dockerfile-check.test.ts` — `gate-reader-scaling-ratio` on 41 Dockerfile shapes for each of 3 readers and on 11 lock shapes; `gate-reader-scaling-ratio-control`; `lock-key-many-deps`. Run on the readers from before #258, 18 of those 52 fail, among them all 16 shapes the fixed limits failed there (docs/progress/2026-11-02-lratio-scaling-ratio.md).
