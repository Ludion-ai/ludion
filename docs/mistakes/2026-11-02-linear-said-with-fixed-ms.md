# 2026-11-02 — "Linear" was said from fixed limits in milliseconds

- **What happened.** #258 wrote in packages/cli/test/dockerfile-check.test.ts that "the lock and Dockerfile readers decide in linear time", and named its tests `gate-reader-linear`. What those tests held was a fixed time at one size of each input: 50 ms, 1 s and 4 s. So the sentence said more than the test, in two ways. It was general, while the same PR's record measured 37,105 ms on one key of about 4,096 characters with 10,000 dependencies and called that "no bound"; `resolveFrom` is quadratic in a key's length, as `lock-key-many-deps` now measures (about 14 times from a key of 1,019 characters to one of 4,034). And a limit at one size does not show how a time grows. On main's code from before #258, `lockEntries` and `lockExtraneous` on 1,000 extraneous keys of 4,096 characters took about 480 ms, under the 1 s limit, so they passed with the quadratic expression still there; from 1,024 characters to 4,096 they grew 12.56 and 13.05 times. The limits also depended on the machine: GitHub's runner went over the first limit on `packageLockInputs`, 1 s, in all 3 tries, and 4 s was then picked "between the two times". A first-principles audit on 2026-11-02 found the sentence and the limits.
- **Why.** The word came from the reasoning behind the fix (an expression with one way to match any key), not from a measurement, and nobody compared it with what the test measures. A limit in milliseconds was the check at hand, as `time-cap-composer-name.test.ts` already used one to stop an exponential expression, which a fixed limit does catch. A quadratic time needs two sizes to be seen.
- **What changed.** `gate-reader-scaling-ratio` measures each reader on the same shape at n and 4n, as the median of up to 7 samples of CPU time taken in turn, and holds the ratio under 8 (a time that grows with n² gives about 16). `gate-reader-scaling-ratio-control` holds that the same measure gives under 8 on a scan and over 8 on a known n², in the same run. The file's first line and its describe names now say "these shapes". `lock-key-many-deps` writes the quadratic time down as it is, a ratio from 8 up to 32, and calls it neither linear nor fixed; the fix is left to another PR, and when it lands those tests fail. The time limits that remain do not decide what is linear: 5 s and 30 s for a sample stop a run that would hang, and 15 s for a measure ends its samples early and fails nothing.
- **The same fault, in this fix's first head.** That head measured the ratio in clock time, and said the samples were taken "in turn, so a slow moment falls on both". Nobody had measured that. Its review, one run each with no reader changed, failed 38 of 57 tests under `--cpus 0.25`, 28 under `--cpuset-cpus 0` with 3 busy loops and 45 under `--cpus 1` with 3 busy loops, where the fixed limits passed in the two of those containers they ran in. So it still depended on the machine, as the limits it replaced did. A short sample can run with no wait while the long one after it takes the whole wait. The ratio now reads CPU time: 0 of 9 runs failed in those containers (95% upper bound 28%), and a real n² in a copy of `officialImage` still failed all 41 shapes in each of them and with no limit (docs/progress/2026-11-02-lratio-scaling-ratio.md). The same head also said its 15 s budget fails a slow reader, which it does not.
- **What this does not catch.** A ratio at one pair of sizes says how a listed shape grew between those sizes: not how another shape grows, and not a term still too small to show at those sizes. Where a reader refuses a file at its first line, the ratio is low because little of the file is read (the lowest are under 2), which is a true measure and not evidence about the rest of the reader. A reader slower by the same factor at both sizes is not caught: a busy wait of 1 s at 16 KiB and 4 s at 64 KiB gave 4.00 and passed, and nothing fails until the guards or the test's own time limit. A time that uses no CPU is not caught either: an `Atomics.wait` that grew with n² gave 3.60 and passed; the readers wait for nothing today. Sentences outside this PR's files still say "linear", among them the comment on `LOCK_PATH` in packages/cli/src/security/judge.ts, packages/cli/src/security/deliver.ts:774, packages/cli/src/security/time-cap.ts:175, line 1 of packages/cli/test/time-cap-composer-name.test.ts (which holds a 50 ms limit) and the `composer-name-regex-linear` test names there, and test names in readonly-rust.test.ts, readonly-framework.test.ts and readonly-registered.test.ts. They were not measured here. The ratio can still fail with no reader changed on a machine unlike those tried: the highest seen on a limited machine was 7.31 against the line of 8, and 0 of 9 is an upper bound of 28%, not zero.
- **Check:** `packages/cli/test/dockerfile-check.test.ts` — `gate-reader-scaling-ratio` on 41 Dockerfile shapes for each of 3 readers and on 11 lock shapes; `gate-reader-scaling-ratio-control`; `lock-key-many-deps`. Run on the readers from before #258, 18 of those 52 fail, among them all 16 shapes the fixed limits failed there (docs/progress/2026-11-02-lratio-scaling-ratio.md).
